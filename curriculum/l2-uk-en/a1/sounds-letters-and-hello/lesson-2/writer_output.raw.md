# Stress: Наголос

In this second lesson, you take the next essential steps into Ukrainian phonetics. You will discover how sounds combine to form syllables (**склади́**), explore how consonant sounds are shaped in the mouth, complete a full pronunciation pass through all 33 letters of the Ukrainian alphabet (**абе́тка**), and learn how stress (**на́голос**) directs your voice and gives words their meaning.

## What Is a Склад?

A **склад** is a syllable. In Ukrainian, every syllable needs a vowel sound. That is why vowels are the "heart" of a syllable.

In Ukrainian schoolbooks (such as Avramenko, 5 клас, с. 84 and Zabolotnyi, 5 клас, с. 87), a syllable is defined as a part of a word pronounced with a single breath push. Because vowels are open, voiced sounds produced without any blockage in the vocal tract, only a vowel sound can form the center of a syllable. Consonants cluster around that vowel core.

This creates the foundational rule taught throughout Ukrainian primary education: a word has as many syllables as it has vowel sounds.

You do not need to count syllables confidently yet. M02 teaches that reading rule. Today, just notice that the vowel sound gives each **склад** its voice.

| Word | What to notice today |
| --- | --- |
| **день** | one clear **е** sound |
| **ма́ма** | two **а** sounds |
| **Приві́т** | **и** and **і** are different vowel sounds |
| **молоко́** | three clean **о** sounds |

Notice how this rule works in practice:
- **день** has one vowel sound (**[е]**), so it is a single-syllable word.
- **ма́ма** has two vowel sounds (**[а]**, **[а]**), so it has two syllables: **ма-ма**.
- **Приві́т** has two vowel sounds (**[и]**, **[і]**), so it has two syllables: **При-віт**.
- **молоко́** has three vowel sounds (**[о]**, **[о]**, **[о]**), so it has three syllables: **мо-ло-ко**.

Now the word **склад** will not surprise you when the workbook previews it and the next module starts reading practice.

## Consonant Sounds

A consonant sound uses a small block or contact in the mouth: lips, teeth, tongue, or throat.

While vowels are pure voice, consonants are formed by creating an obstruction. As Bolshakova (1 клас, с. 24) explains to young Ukrainian school children: consonants rustle, hiss, and buzz. Air cannot pass freely; the lips, tongue, or teeth meet to shape the sound.

Try four:

- **[м]** - close your lips and hum.
- **[п]** - close your lips and release without humming.
- **[т]** - touch your tongue behind your teeth.
- **[н]** - touch your tongue and let sound pass through your nose.

Ukrainian has 32 consonant sounds and 22 letters that usually mark consonants. Some consonants have hard and soft versions. You do not need the full system today, but you do need five first clues:

Vowels give a syllable its voice. Consonants make the clear shape around that voice: lips, teeth, tongue, or throat do the work.

| Sign or letter | Beginner meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Ь ь** | **м'який знак**; no sound of its own |
| **Г г** | Ukrainian h-like sound |
| **Ґ ґ** | hard **g** sound |
| **Щ щ** | two sounds together: **[шч]** |
| **'** | **апо́строф**; a separation mark. M03 teaches the details |

Example:

**день** - day

The final **ь** does not add a new vowel. It tells you to make **н** soft.

The letters **Я, Ю, Є** have two common jobs: at the start of a word they can show **[й] + vowel**, and after a consonant they can point to softness plus one vowel. **Ї** is the strict exception: always **[йі]**.

A crucial rule of Ukrainian pronunciation (as documented in standard phonetic guides such as Pohribnyi 1992, с. 11): voiced consonants in Ukrainian remain voiced at the end of words and syllables. Unlike some neighboring languages, Ukrainian speakers do not devoice final consonants (for example, the **д** in words stays clearly voiced). Keeping voiced consonants crisp and distinct preserves the integrity of Ukrainian words.

## Stress: Наголос

The little mark in **Приві́т** is the stress mark. Ukrainian stress is called **на́голос**.

In Ukrainian linguistics (Avramenko, 5 клас, с. 88; Zabolotnyi, 5 клас, с. 90), stress is defined as pronouncing one syllable with greater vocal force. The syllable that receives this emphasis is the stressed syllable, while the other syllables in the word are unstressed.

Ukrainian stress is free, meaning it is not locked to a single fixed position (such as always on the first syllable), and mobile, meaning it can move between different forms of a word.

Stress tells you which syllable is strongest:

| Word | How to say it |
| --- | --- |
| **Приві́т** | stress on **ві́т** |
| **ма́ма** | stress on the first **ма́** |
| **молоко́** | stress on **ко́** |
| **До́брий день** | stress on **До́-** |

Listen to how speakers stress these words naturally when greeting one another:

> **А́нна**: До́брий день!
> **Марко́**: До́брий день!

| Ukrainian | English support | Stress pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **До́брий день!** | Hello! / Good day! | Stress falls on the first syllable **До́-** |

Sometimes stress changes meaning. You do not need to use these words yet, but notice the pattern:

| Stress | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **за́мок** | castle |
| **замо́к** | lock |
| **бра́ти** | brothers |
| **брати́** | to take |

For this course, new Ukrainian words usually show stress so you know what to say aloud.

An essential pronunciation habit to practice from your very first lessons: Ukrainian unstressed **о** always stays clean, rounded, and pure. In **молоко́**, every single **о** sounds like **[о]**. It never reduces to an obscure English "uh" or an *a*-like sound. Keeping unstressed vowels clear and placing the stress accurately on the correct syllable will ensure your Ukrainian sounds authentic and natural.

### Підсумок уроку — Lesson summary

In this lesson, you explored how the basic building blocks of Ukrainian speech operate together:
- Syllables (**склади́**): every syllable is centered on a vowel sound; a word contains exactly as many syllables as it has vowel sounds.
- Consonants: shaped by contact in the mouth (lips, teeth, tongue, throat), with special letters like **Ґ** ([g]), **Щ** ([шч]), **Г** (h-like), and the silent modifier **Ь**.
- Alphabet pass: you encountered all 33 letters of the Ukrainian alphabet (**абе́тка**) alongside real example words.
- Stress (**на́голос**): Ukrainian stress is free and mobile, highlights the prominent syllable, and can distinguish entirely different meanings (such as **за́мок** vs **замо́к**).
- Vowel purity: unstressed **о** remains a clean, rounded sound in every syllable.

In Lesson 3, you will learn how Ukrainian sounds flow melodiously together without awkward clusters (**милозву́чність**) and practice your first full spoken conversations.
