# Sounds, Letters, and Hello

You do not need to read Ukrainian yet. This English-led module uses Ukrainian in small phrases so you can listen, repeat, notice, and use the first phrases safely.

Use this first pass as orientation: one reliable sound rule, a small set of greetings, and enough alphabet awareness to start the workbook without guessing.

By the end, you can:

- say hello, goodbye, and a simple "How are you?";
- answer with **До́бре**, **Чудо́во**, or **Норма́льно**;
- ask and answer a name question;
- explain the difference between a sound and a letter;
- recognize all 33 Ukrainian letters at first-pass level;
- know what **склад** means before the next module asks you to count syllables;
- avoid the first pronunciation traps: blurred **о**, confusing **и** and **і**, adding a sound after **ь**, and turning final voiced consonants into voiceless ones.

## Sound First, Letter Second

Keep this rule in mind from the very first minute:

| You hear and say | You see and write |
| --- | --- |
| a **sound** | a **letter** |
| **звук** — sound | **лі́тера** — letter |

A **sound** belongs to your ear and mouth. A **letter** belongs to your eyes and hand. In Ukrainian schoolbooks, this distinction is taught from the earliest grades as the foundation of reading and writing.

> **Золоте́ пра́вило з підру́чника (Заболо́тний, 5 клас, с. 83) — Golden rule from the textbook (Zabolotnyi, Grade 5, p. 83):**
> «Зву́ки ми чу́ємо й вимовля́ємо, а бу́кви ба́чимо й пи́шемо».
> *"We hear and pronounce sounds, but we see and write letters."*

Why does this matter so much? Because letters on the page are silent graphic symbols. They do not have a voice of their own. As Litvinova (5 клас, с. 130) emphasizes when training young learners: strictly speaking, there is no such thing as a "vowel letter" (*голосна́ лі́тера* — vowel letter). Sounds are vowels or consonants; letters are simply marks on paper that represent those sounds.

Three numbers define the architecture of Ukrainian phonetics:

| Fact | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **33 letters** | the Ukrainian alphabet, or **абе́тка** |
| **38 sounds** | what native speakers actually produce and hear |
| **6 vowel sounds** | the open, resonant sounds you can sing |

The number of letters and the number of sounds are not identical because Ukrainian letters perform specific phonetic jobs:

- Four letters (**Я, Ю, Є, Ї**) can point to combinations of two sounds (a consonant plus a vowel) or signal that a preceding consonant is soft.
- One letter (**Ь**, the soft sign) has no sound of its own at all; it exists purely to soften the consonant before it.
- Many consonant letters can represent either a hard sound or a softened sound depending on what follows them.

Ukrainian primary textbooks (such as Zaharijchuk, 1 клас, с. 13) introduce visual models to help learners track sounds: vowel sounds are marked with a circle `[•]`, and hard consonant sounds are marked with a dash `[–]`.

Say this foundational rule aloud:

**Звук = I hear it and pronounce it. Лі́тера = I see it and write it.**

Start with the six clean vowel sounds: **[а] [о] [у] [е] [и] [і]**. They are your primary guide because every Ukrainian syllable needs a vowel sound.

## Your First Word

Your very first Ukrainian word is:

**Приві́т!** — Hi!

Use **Приві́т!** with classmates, friends, family, or people your own age. Treat it as one whole lexical phrase today.

Read how two speakers greet each other:

> **А́нна**: Приві́т!
> **Марко́**: Приві́т!

| Ре́пліка украї́нською | English support | Social context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Приві́т!** | Hi! / Hello! | Everyday, informal greeting for friends and peers |

Say it slowly, breaking it into its two syllables:

**При-ві́т**

Now look closely at the building blocks:

| Letter | Sound | Type | Sound category |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **П** | [п] | letter | consonant |
| **р** | [р] | letter | consonant |
| **и** | [и] | letter | vowel |
| **в** | [в] | letter | consonant |
| **і** | [і] | letter | vowel |
| **т** | [т] | letter | consonant |

Notice what this breakdown reveals: **Приві́т** contains two vowel sounds and four consonant sounds.

Notice also that **и** and **і** are two completely distinct vowel sounds in Ukrainian. The letter **и** marks the open, retracted vowel **[и]**, while the letter **і** marks the bright, close vowel **[і]**. Listening carefully to each vowel prevents the common mistake of blurring them together.

## Six Vowel Sounds, Ten Letters

Ukrainian has six basic vowel sounds:

**[а] [о] [у] [е] [и] [і]**

A vowel sound (*голосни́й звук*) is formed purely with voice. When you pronounce a vowel, your vocal cords vibrate and the air stream passes freely through the mouth without encountering any obstacle from your lips, teeth, or tongue.

> **Пра́вило з підру́чника (Большако́ва, 1 клас, с. 24) — Rule from the textbook (Bolshakova, Grade 1, p. 24):**
> «Голосні́ почу́єш в пісні́, і у те́мному у лі́сі, і коли́ диву́єшся, і коли́ милу́єшся. Ле́гко вимовля́ються, ве́село співа́ються!»
> *"You will hear vowels in song, and in the dark forest, and when you wonder, and when you admire. Easily pronounced, cheerfully sung!"*

Because the air flow is unobstructed, you can hold and sing any vowel sound:

- **а-а-а**
- **о-о-о**
- **у-у-у**
- **і-і-і**

To pronounce each sound accurately, observe how your mouth changes shape:

- **[а]** — wide open jaw, relaxed tongue resting flat.
- **[о]** — lips rounded into a neat circle, jaw moderately open.
- **[у]** — lips pushed forward into a small, tight circle, like preparing to whistle.
- **[е]** — lips unrounded, mouth moderately open, tongue resting mid-mouth.
- **[и]** — lips relaxed, tongue pulled slightly back and down; never replace it with English short *i*.
- **[і]** — wide smile, lips spread, tongue arched high toward the front roof of the mouth.

While there are only six vowel sounds, the Ukrainian alphabet contains ten letters that mark vowel sounds:

| Simple vowel letters | Special (iotated) vowel letters |
| --- | --- |
| **А О У Е И І** | **Я Ю Є Ї** |

You do not need to master all the rules for the special letters today. Simply recognize their primary roles for now:

| Letter | Today, notice |
| --- | --- |
| **Я** | can represent **[йа]** or soften the consonant before **[а]** |
| **Ю** | can represent **[йу]** or soften the consonant before **[у]** |
| **Є** | can represent **[йе]** or soften the consonant before **[е]** |
| **Ї** | always represents two sounds together: **[йі]** |

Now examine seven essential Ukrainian words that showcase these vowel sounds:

| Word | Meaning | What to notice about the vowels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ма́ма** | mother | two clear **[а]** sounds |
| **та́то** | father | the open vowel **[а]** followed by clean **[о]** |
| **молоко́** | milk | three clean, unreduced **[о]** sounds |
| **о́ко** | eye | two clean **[о]** sounds |
| **дім** | house, home | one sharp **[і]** sound |
| **ніс** | nose | one sharp **[і]** sound |
| **сон** | sleep, dream | one resonant **[о]** sound |

Pay special attention to the pronunciation of **о**. In Ukrainian, unstressed **о** stays clean and rounded. It never blurs into an English-style reduced sound or an *a*-sound. In **мо-ло-ко́**, each syllable retains a crisp, distinct **[о]**.

### Пі́дсумок уро́ку — Lesson summary

In this opening lesson, you established the central principle of Ukrainian phonetics:
- Sounds are what you hear and pronounce; letters are what you see and write.
- Ukrainian has 33 letters in its alphabet, but 38 sounds in actual speech.
- The six vowel sounds — **[а] [о] [у] [е] [и] [і]** — are produced by pure voice without mouth obstruction.
- Ten letters mark vowel sounds: six basic letters and four special iotated letters.
- Ukrainian vowels remain pure and clear, especially unstressed **[о]**.
- Your first word, **Приві́т!**, unites two distinct vowels and four consonants into an everyday greeting.

In Lesson 2, you will examine consonant sounds, explore syllables (*скла́ди*), complete a full pronunciation pass of all 33 letters, and discover how stress (*на́голос*) directs your voice.
