# Інтона́ція та мело́дика ре́чення: Тве́рдження, пита́ння, о́клик

**Це ка́ва. Це ка́ва? Як га́рно!** — sentence melody and intonation in Ukrainian.

Words alone do not convey full human intent. The exact same sequence of words can communicate a calm fact, a doubtful question, or an emotional outburst depending on the musical curve of your voice. In Ukrainian linguistics, this vocal curve across an entire utterance is called **інтона́ція** (intonation) or **мело́дика ре́чення** (sentence melody).

In Lessons 4.1 and 4.2, you focused on word-level stress (**слове́сний на́голос**). Now you step back to look at the entire sentence. Ukrainian is a deeply musical language where intonation patterns are clear, expressive, and directly reflected by punctuation marks.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- Master the four core sentence melodies defined by Ukrainian grammar (Авраменко, 5 клас, с. 19):
  1. **Розповідна́ інтона́ція ↘:** a calm, falling contour for statements ending in a period;
  2. **Пита́льна інтона́ція зага́льного пита́ння (так/ні) ↗:** a sharp rising contour on the key focus word;
  3. **Пита́льна інтона́ція з пита́льним сло́вом ↘:** a falling contour when the sentence begins with **хто**, **що**, **де**, **коли́**, or **як**;
  4. **Окли́чна інтона́ція ↘↘:** an expressive peak with an emphatic fall for greetings, exclamations, and thanks;
- Apply appropriate sentence melody to all previously mastered social etiquette formulas (**Приві́т!**, **До́брий день!**, **Як спра́ви?**, **До поба́чення!**, **Дя́кую!**);
- Understand the role of logical stress (**логі́чний на́голос**) in emphasizing the communicative center of a sentence;
- Use grammatical terminology accurately, appreciating how written symbols (**бу́ква** та **лі́тера**) combine with punctuation marks to guide voice inflection;
- Perform living conversational exchanges with authentic Ukrainian melodic contours.

:::tip
Punctuation marks on the page are musical notation for your voice:
- A period (**.**) signals a gentle fall **↘**;
- A question mark (**?**) signals a rise **↗** for yes/no questions, but a fall **↘** if a question word is present;
- An exclamation mark (**!**) signals an expressive rise-fall **↘↘**.
:::

## Чоти́ри мелоди́чні моде́лі: Кра́пка, знак пита́ння, знак о́клику

In Ukrainian syntax, sentences are classified according to the purpose of utterance into **розповідні́** (declarative / statements), **пита́льні** (interrogative / questions), and **спонука́льні** (imperative / requests and commands). Any of these types can also become **окли́чними** (exclamatory) if spoken with heightened emotion.

For everyday A1 communication, we use four standard melodic models:

1. **Тве́рдження (Statement) — Спадна́ інтона́ція ↘:**
   When delivering neutral information, your voice starts at a comfortable mid-pitch and gently descends on the final stressed vowel, ending in a low tone before the final pause:
   - **Це ка́ва. ↘** — *This is coffee.*
   - **Марко́ вдо́ма. ↘** — *Marko is at home.*
   - **Тут наш дім. ↘** — *Here is our home.*
   - **Марі́я чита́є кни́гу. ↘** — *Mariia reads a book.*

2. **Зага́льне пита́ння (Yes/No Question) — Висхідна́ інтона́ція ↗:**
   When asking a question that requires a "yes" (**так**) or "no" (**ні**) answer, word order does not change in Ukrainian. The question is signaled entirely by voice inflection! The pitch rises sharply and steeply on the stressed syllable of the word you are asking about:
   - **Це ка́ва? ↗** (pitch spikes on **ка́-**) — *Is this coffee?*
   - **Марко́ вдо́ма? ↗** (pitch spikes on **вдо́-**) — *Is Marko at home?*
   - **Це апте́ка? ↗** (pitch spikes on **-те́-**) — *Is this a pharmacy?*

3. **О́клик (Exclamation) — Си́льна спадна́ інтона́ція ↘↘:**
   When expressing admiration, joy, surprise, or an urgent greeting, the voice leaps to a high peak on the stressed syllable of the exclamatory word and drops steeply:
   - **Як га́рно! ↘↘** — *How beautiful! / How lovely!*
   - **Чудо́во! ↘↘** — *Wonderful! / Excellent!*
   - **Яка́ краса́! ↘↘** — *What beauty!*

| Тип ре́чення — Sentence type | Моде́ль — Model | Інтона́ція — Voice contour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| тве́рдження (statement) | **Це ка́ва.** | спадна́ **↘** |
| зага́льне пита́ння (yes/no question) | **Це ка́ва?** | висхідна́ **↗** |
| о́клик (exclamation) | **Як га́рно!** | сильні́ша спадна́ **↘↘** |

## Пита́ння так/ні про́ти пита́нь із пита́льним сло́вом

This distinction is the most important intonational rule for foreign learners of Ukrainian:

A yes/no question rises (**↗**), but a question containing an interrogative question word (**пита́льне сло́во**) **falls** (**↘**)!

Why does a question with a question word fall? Because the question word itself (**хто**, **що**, **де**, **коли́**, **як**) stands at the beginning of the sentence and already carries primary communicative emphasis. The listener already knows what information is sought from the very first word. Therefore, the melody starts high on the question word and steadily falls towards the end of the sentence:

- **Що це? ↘** — *What is this?* (voice falls on **це**)
- **Хто це? ↘** — *Who is this?* (voice falls on **це**)
- **Де метро́? ↘** — *Where is the metro?* (voice falls on **мет-ро́**)
- **Де апте́ка? ↘** — *Where is the pharmacy?* (voice falls on **ап-те́-ка**)
- **Як спра́ви? ↘** — *How are things?* (voice falls on **спра́-ви**)
- **Коли́ уро́к? ↘** — *When is the lesson?* (voice falls on **у-ро́к**)

Compare these two direct contrasts:
- **Це метро́? ↗** (No question word → sharp rise at the end: *Is this the metro?*)
- **Де метро́? ↘** (Contains question word **де** → gentle fall at the end: *Where is the metro?*)

- **Це ка́ва? ↗** (No question word → sharp rise: *Is this coffee?*)
- **Що це? ↘** (Contains question word **що** → gentle fall: *What is this?*)

Every Ukrainian learner must master this difference. Do not let your voice rise on **Де апте́ка? ↘** or **Як спра́ви? ↘**; keep the sentence ending calm and falling.

| --- | --- | --- |
| **хто** (who) | **Хто це?** | спадна́ **↘** |
| **що** (what) | **Що це?** | спадна́ **↘** |
| **де** (where) | **Де метро́?** | спадна́ **↘** |

## Мело́дика етике́тних фо́рмул та віта́нь

All the conversational etiquette formulas you have learned in Modules 1, 2, and 3 now take on their full acoustic polish. Notice how the combination of letters (**бу́ква** чи **лі́тера**) and intonation conveys warmth, politeness, and respect:

1. **Приві́т! ↘↘** (Informal greeting)
   The tone begins with warm vocal energy and drops expressively on the stressed syllable **-ві́т**:
   *«Приві́т! Ра́дий тебе́ ба́чити! ↘↘ — Hi! Glad to see you!»*

2. **До́брий день! ↘** / **До́брого ра́нку! ↘** (Formal greetings)
   These standard daily greetings carry a composed, polite falling contour:
   *«До́брий день! ↘ — Good day! / Good afternoon!»*
   *«До́брого ра́нку! ↘ — Good morning!»*

3. **Як спра́ви? ↘** (Conversational inquiry)
   Because **як** is an interrogative adverb, the formal model contour is falling:
   *«Приві́т, Ма́рку! Як спра́ви? ↘ — Hi, Marko! How are things?»*

4. **Дя́кую! ↘↘** / **Щи́ро дя́кую! ↘↘** (Gratitude)
   Expressive gratitude starts on a bright, elevated pitch on **дя́-** and resolves downwards:
   *«Щи́ро дя́кую за допомо́гу! ↘↘ — Thank you sincerely for your help!»*

5. **До поба́чення! ↘** / **На все до́бре! ↘** (Farewells)
   Polite goodbyes feature a smooth, calming descent:
   *«До поба́чення! ↘ — Goodbye!»*
   *«На все до́бре! ↘ — All the best!»*

| **Приві́т!** | Hi! / Hello! | окли́чна спадна́ **↘↘** |
| **До́брий день!** | Good day! | спокі́йна спадна́ **↘** |
| **До́брого ра́нку!** | Good morning! | спокі́йна спадна́ **↘** |

## Логі́чний на́голос та живи́й діало́г

Within a sentence, one word often carries special communicative importance. This is called **логі́чний на́голос** — logical stress. When a speaker applies logical stress, they give that specific word greater prominence by raising the pitch, increasing vocal volume, and pausing slightly before or after it.

Consider this three-way contrast:
- **`Це` ка́ва? ↗** (Is *this* coffee, or something else?)
- **Це `ка́ва`? ↗** (Is this *coffee*, or tea?)
- **А у `тебе́`? ↗** (And what about *you*?)

In reciprocal questions like **«А в тебе́?»** or **«А у тебе́ як спра́ви?»**, the logical stress naturally highlights the pronoun because you are turning the inquiry back to your conversational partner.

Now listen to and practice a complete interactive exchange between **Окса́на** and **Марко́** on a Kyiv street, observing every intonational arrow:

> **Окса́на:** — До́брий день, Ма́рку! ↘
>
> **Марко́:** — Приві́т, Окса́но! ↘↘ Як спра́ви? ↘
>
> **Окса́на:** — Дя́кую, чудо́во! ↘↘ А в тебе́? ↗
>
> **Марко́:** — У мене́ теж усе́ до́бре. ↘ Підкажи́, будь ла́ска: це нова́ апте́ка? ↗
>
> **Окса́на:** — Так, це апте́ка. ↘ А де метро́? ↘
>
> **Марко́:** — Ось ста́нція метро́, по́руч. ↘
>
> **Окса́на:** — Як га́рно! ↘↘ Щи́ро дя́кую! ↘↘
>
> **Марко́:** — Будь ла́ска! ↘ До поба́чення! ↘
>
> **Окса́на:** — На все до́бре! ↘
