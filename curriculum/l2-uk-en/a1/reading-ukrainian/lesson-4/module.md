# Голосна́ Е, шипля́чі та пе́рші віта́ння: Бу́кви Е, З, Ж, Ш, Х — Vowel Е, Hushing Sounds, and First Greetings: Letters Е, З, Ж, Ш, Х

Ласка́во про́симо до четве́ртого уро́ку! — Welcome to the fourth lesson! Over the first three lessons, you mastered 18 letters (**А, О, У, М, Л, С, Н, К, И, Р, Б, В, Д, І, П, Т, Г, Ґ**) and learned to decode words smoothly by counting vowels to find each word's beats.

In this lesson, you add five essential letters: the clean, open vowel **Е**, the buzzing sound **З**, two firm sounds **Ж** and **Ш** (which stay “zh” and “sh”), and the soft throat-hiss **Х**. This expands your reading repertoire to 23 letters — more than two thirds of the Ukrainian alphabet! Most excitingly, every single letter required to read Ukraine's two most cherished everyday greetings — **Приві́т!** and **До́брого ра́нку!** — is now fully unlocked.

By the end of this 60-minute lesson, you will be able to:

- **Вимовля́ти чи́стий голосни́й [е]** — pronounce a clean, open Ukrainian **е** (like "e" in *pet*, without a "y"-glide);
- **Розрізня́ти дзвінки́й [з] та глухи́й [х]** — pronounce buzzing **з** (like "z" in *zoo*) and soft throat-hiss **х** (like Scottish *loch* or "ch" in *Bach*);
- **Трима́ти зву́ки [ж] та [ш] тверди́ми** — **ж** and **ш** stay “zh” and “sh”; they do not go soft in these words (**жа́ба**, **шко́ла**, **ка́ша**, **ти́ша**);
- **Чита́ти та вжива́ти пе́рші віта́ння** — confidently read, pronounce, and understand Ukraine's two classic greetings: **Приві́т!** and **До́брого ра́нку!**;
- **Чита́ти нови́й пласт побуто́вої ле́ксики** — decode common Ukrainian words like **хліб**, **ха́та**, **зуб**, **село́**, **мо́ре**, **не́бо**, and **зеле́на**.

## Голосни́й звук [е] та бу́ква Е

Let us welcome our sixth simple vowel: **Е**.

| Бу́ква — Letter | На́зва — Name | Звук — Sound | Характери́стика — Phonetic nature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Е, е** | е | **[е]** | Чи́стий відкри́тий голосни́й — Clean open vowel (like *pet*, no "y"-glide) |

In Ukrainian, **[е] is a clean, open vowel**. Unlike English *yes* or Russian *е*, Ukrainian **е** does not add a *y*-glide ([ye]) after consonants. Consonants before **Е** stay plain and hard:

- **се-ло́ = село́** (village);
- **не́-бо = не́бо** (sky);
- **ле-ле́-ка = леле́ка** (stork, 3 beats);
- **ме-тро́ = метро́** (metro / subway);
- **те-а́тр = теа́тр** (theater, 2 beats);
- **зе-ле́-на = зеле́на** (green, feminine).

Notice how open and relaxed your jaw is when pronouncing **се-ло́** or **не́-бо**.

## Дзвінкі́ та глухі́: Бу́кви З і Х

Now meet two distinct consonants:

| Бу́ква — Letter | На́зва — Name | Звук — Sound | О́пис — Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **З, з** | зе | **[z]** | Дзвінки́й звук (як англі́йське *z*) — Buzzing sound like *zoo* (voiced pair to **С**) |
| **Х, х** | ха | **[x]** | Глухи́й звук (як *ch* у *Bach* або *loch*) — Soft throat-hiss like "ch" in *Bach* or Scottish *loch* |

The letter **З** is the buzzing, voiced partner of the voiceless hiss **С** you learned in Module 1:
- **зуб** (tooth, 1 beat; final **б** still sounds like **б**, not **п**).

The letter **Х** is a soft breath sound from the back of the mouth/throat, like Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*:
- **хліб** (bread, 1 beat; final **б** still sounds like **б**, not **п**);
- **ха́-та = ха́та** (traditional house / cottage).

## Тверді́ зву́ки: Бу́кви Ж і Ш

In Ukrainian standard pronunciation, **ж** and **ш** stay “zh” and “sh”; they do not go soft in these words:

| Бу́ква — Letter | На́зва — Name | Звук — Sound | Пра́вило — Reading rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ж, ж** | же | **[ʒ]** | Дзвінки́й звук (як *s* у *treasure*) — Buzzing "zh" (stays hard before vowels) |
| **Ш, ш** | ша | **[ʃ]** | Глухи́й звук (як *sh* у *shoe*) — Whispering "sh" (stays hard before vowels) |

:::important
**Зву́ки [ж] та [ш] лиша́ються тверди́ми — ж and ш stay “zh” and “sh”**
In Ukrainian, **ж** and **ш** stay “zh” and “sh”; they do not go soft in these words (**жа́ба**, **шко́ла**, **ка́ша**, **ти́ша**). Keep your tongue firm and resonant:
- **жа́-ба = жа́ба** (frog);
- **шко́-ла = шко́ла** (school);
- **ка́-ша = ка́ша** (porridge);
- **ти́-ша = ти́ша** (quiet / silence);
- **наш** (our, 1 beat).

Never pronounce **ка́ша** or **жа́ба** with soft, slushy sounds. Keep them deep, firm, and resonant!
:::

## Пе́рші віта́ння: Приві́т! та До́брого ра́нку!

Now comes the most rewarding milestone of your reading journey! In Module 1, greetings were deliberately withheld so you would never memorize unanalyzed conversational phrases. Today, every single letter of Ukraine's two classic greetings is fully unlocked:

### 1. **Приві́т!** — Hi! / Hello! (informal)
Let us decode it letter by letter:
- **П** — crisp stop like *p* in *spin*
- **Р** — rolled / tapped r
- **И** — relaxed "ih" vowel (like *bit*)
- **В** — soft *v* before *і*
- **І** — smiling "ee" vowel (carries the word's acute stress: **При-ві́т**)
- **Т** — crisp stop *t*

Two beats (syllables): **При-ві́т!**. Use **Приві́т!** when greeting peers, friends, classmates, and family.

### 2. **До́брого ра́нку!** — Good morning! (universal)
Let us decode each word:
- **До́б-ро-го**: 3 beats (**до́б-ро-го**), stress on the first **о**. Letters: Д, О, Б, Р, О, Г, О. Notice: final **-го** has the soft throat sound **Г**; and unstressed **о** stays **о**!
- **ра́н-ку**: 2 beats (**ра́н-ку**), stress on **а**. Letters: Р, А, Н, К, У.

Use **До́брого ра́нку!** in the morning with anyone — teachers, neighbors, colleagues, or strangers on the street.
