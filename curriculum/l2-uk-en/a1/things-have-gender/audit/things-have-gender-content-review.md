## Fix applied during this review

**CRITICAL — corrupted possessive pronoun (`воя́` for `моя́`) — FIXED in place.**

`lesson-1/module.md` and the published `site/src/content/docs/a1/things-have-gender/1.mdx`
both contained, in the Lesson-1 anchor `:::tip` (the very first callout in the module, the one
that fixes the мій/моя/моє pattern in the learner's head):

> Ask, "What phrase travels with this noun: **мій**, **воя́**, or **моє́**?"

`воя́` is not the feminine possessive. `mcp__sources__verify_word` confirms `воя` exists in
VESUM only as an archaic/dialectal genitive singular of `вій` ("eyelash") — a real form,
totally unrelated in meaning, and invisible to the VESUM/forbidden-words gate because it *is*
a valid Ukrainian word, just not the one the sentence needs. The archived `a1-v1` original had
the correct triad (`мій`, `моя`, `моє`) at this exact spot, so this is a corruption introduced
during the upgrade-split/stress-annotation pass, not a plan-side error. It is also the concrete
referent behind `lesson_qg.json`'s `"воя́→во́я"` stress-contradiction line and the
`"original paragraph not preserved verbatim"` blocking diagnostic — the deterministic gate
correctly detected *that* something changed but mischaracterized it as a stress mismatch; the
real defect is a wrong word.

Notably, AGY's own LLM QG had already caught this independently and correctly, four separate
times, across `module_coherence.json` and the per-lesson `naturalness`/`pedagogical` QG passes
(`issue_ids: ["CORRUPTED_UKRAINIAN_FORM"]` / `"LEARNER_FACING_TYPO"`, severities major/low,
dimension verdicts `REVISE`) — yet the module's `aggregate.verdict: "REVISE"` was overridden by
an `aggregate.terminal_verdict: "PASS"`, and the typo shipped to `build_status: validated`
content anyway. **This is a process finding, not just a content one**: the terminal-verdict
aggregation is letting a self-identified, correctly-diagnosed content defect through. Whoever
owns that aggregation logic should look at why a `REVISE` on two dimensions (`pedagogical`,
`naturalness`) collapses to a `PASS` terminal verdict.

I corrected `моя́` in both `lesson-1/module.md` and the published `1.mdx` (the two live,
learner-facing copies) with VESUM evidence in hand, per this dispatch's "CRITICAL errors you
can fix in-place" allowance. I did **not** touch `module_coherence.json`, `lesson_qg.json`, or
any `writer_output.raw.md` / `llm-qg-*` file — those are the historical build record and should
be regenerated by a real rebuild, not hand-patched.

Because the module still needs a rebuild to regenerate the stale gate dumps and resolve the
issues below, I have graded this **F** rather than retroactively upgrading it — the deployable
artifact as originally submitted for review had a live CRITICAL, and a second HIGH (below)
remains unfixed by design (needs a writer pass, not a one-word patch).

## Lesson-Split Checks

### 1. Cumulative first-use vocabulary — **HIGH violation**

**Finding:** Lesson 1's own activities drill nine room-vocabulary nouns that are not
introduced (no gloss, no vocabulary.yaml entry) until **Lesson 2**:

- **act-2** (inline, injected right after the first vocabulary table): group-sort with
  `стіле́ць, телефо́н, комп'ю́тер` (він/мій), `кімна́та, ла́мпа, ру́чка` (вона́/моя́),
  `лі́жко, крі́сло, дзе́ркало` (воно́/моє́) — **9 of 12 items** are Lesson-2-only vocabulary.
- **act-5** (inline quiz): includes `телефо́н`, `кімна́та`, `лі́жко` among its 6 items.
- **act-w1** (workbook group-sort): includes `телефо́н, зо́шит, ключ` / `ла́мпа, кімна́та,
  ру́чка` / `лі́жко, крі́сло, дзе́ркало, фо́то` — again mostly Lesson-2 vocabulary.

None of `стілець, лампа, кімната, ручка, ліжко, крісло, дзеркало, зошит, ключ, фото` appear
in `lesson-1/vocabulary.yaml`, and several (лампа, дзеркало, крісло, зошит, ключ) are not
transparent English cognates a beginner could guess. This is **not** a pre-existing defect:
in the single-file `a1-v1` original, all of this vocabulary lived in one shared
`vocabulary.yaml` and the learner read straight through to the "Предмети навколо" section
(which formally introduces these words) in the same continuous sitting before ever finishing
the module's activities. The lesson split turned that into three standalone 60-minute
sessions with per-lesson vocab files — but the writer reused the original activity content
verbatim without re-scoping which lesson it belongs to, so a learner who does Lesson 1 as a
discrete session is drilled on words they have never seen glossed, in the lesson they haven't
reached yet.

**Recommendation:** either (a) move act-2/act-5/act-w1 (or at least their Lesson-2-vocabulary
items) to Lesson 2, or (b) add these nouns to `lesson-1/vocabulary.yaml` with glosses if the
writer wants them drilled in L1. (a) is cleaner and keeps L2's own vocabulary introduction
meaningful.

### 2. Dialogue floor (known residual — writer/format issue found, not a floor problem)

Gate: `l2_exposure_floor` requires ≥13 `uk_dialogue_lines` per lesson; observed 5/0/0.

I traced the root cause: **Lesson 1** got a brand-new `Марко́`/`Окса́на` exchange wrapped in a
real `<DialogueBox>` component (5 lines, correctly counted). **Lessons 2 and 3** carry the
*same* dialogue content that existed in the original archive (`Марі́я`/`Оле́нка`, 10 lines
total across two exchanges in L2; none in L3), but it is still rendered as a plain ` ```text `
fenced code block, never converted to `<DialogueBox>` — both `2.mdx` and `3.mdx` `import
DialogueBox` but never use it. That is almost certainly why the counter reads exactly **0**
for both lessons: it is very likely counting `DialogueBox` exchanges, not fenced-code dialogue
text.

**This changes the recommendation:** the fix is not "invent more dialogue from scratch" — Lesson
2 already has ~10 real dialogue lines sitting in the wrong markup. Converting the existing
Марія/Оленка exchanges to `<DialogueBox>` (mirroring Lesson 1's new pattern) should move Lesson
2 close to the 13-line floor for near-zero new writing. Lesson 3 has no dialogue at all today
(only declarative he-is/she-is pairs) and would still need a short new exchange (4–6 lines,
e.g. two people discussing each other's professions) even after the format fix.

I do **not** recommend lowering the floor or running it on the module union instead of
per-lesson. The whole point of the 60-minute lesson-split is that each lesson is now a
standalone session; a per-lesson floor is what guarantees every session — not just the
module as a whole — models spoken Ukrainian. Collapsing to a module-level union would let
Lessons 2 and 3 (two-thirds of the module's session time) ship with zero modeled dialogue,
which is a real learner-experience regression versus the single-file original (which had
dialogue throughout, just not chunked). **Recommendation: writer should (1) convert L2's
existing dialogue to `DialogueBox` format, (2) add a short new dialogue to L3.**

### 4. No named narrator / named people only inside dialogues — LOW finding

## Grade Justification

One CRITICAL (a corrupted word in the module's very first teaching anchor, already
self-diagnosed by the writer's own QG but shipped anyway due to a terminal-verdict aggregation
bug) and one HIGH (a genuine, split-specific vocabulary-sequencing regression affecting a core
Lesson-1 activity) are both real defects a learner would hit directly. Per the auto-fail rule
("any CRITICAL issue"), this is graded **F** as submitted. I fixed the CRITICAL in place with
clear VESUM evidence because it was a trivial, unambiguous one-word patch, but the HIGH
vocabulary-sequencing issue needs a writer pass (moving/rescoping activities), not a one-line
fix, and the dialogue-floor root cause (MEDIUM) needs a rebuild to verify. None of this reflects
a fundamentally broken module — decolonization (9.3), engagement (8.5) and tone (8.8) scores
from AGY's own QG are well earned, the plan's objectives are all covered, and the split's
lesson-recap/writing-prompt scaffolding is a genuine improvement over the single-file original.
Recommend: writer addresses HIGH-1 and the dialogue-format MEDIUM, then a full rebuild to
regenerate gate dumps and re-review before undrafting.
