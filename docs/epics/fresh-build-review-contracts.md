## The gap
The fresh-build design has ten gates (§6). Nine are deterministic. The tenth is one line: *"review — cross-family content review (LLM) — the only non-deterministic gate"*. Nothing says what that reviewer receives, what it judges, what it may not do, or what it returns. Meanwhile:
- today's `content-review` skill reviews a **whole module** and reads `plans/<level>/<slug>.yaml` — the v1 plan path the new engine must never read (§7.4);
- the design already **delegates four checks to "the cross-family plan review"** without binding any reviewer to them: the short-module recap shape (§2 rule 1b), "does the title describe the job" (rule 5), quantities stated in a title (§2a), and Ukrainian word forms quoted inside plan prose (§2a, rule 7);
- R-35 changes what a reviewer's language claim is worth: a claim needs its source, and the old prompt does not ask for one.

## Principles (both contracts)
1. **A reviewer judges only what a script cannot.** It runs after the deterministic gates are green and receives their report. It never re-counts words, activities, immersion share, coverage or inventory by eye. **Ownership is explicit (r4):** for every area the deterministic side owns *shape and existence* and the reviewer owns *whether it serves the lesson* — closing recap: allowed shape and empty introductions (script) versus whether the short shape serves this module (reviewer); activities: type allowlist, practice linkage and calibrated counts (script) versus whether `focus`, `model` and instruction test the intended learning (reviewer); inventory and sequence: declared introductions and earlier-use relations (script) versus whether the prose teaches or assumes something else (reviewer); evidence: id existence and locks (script) versus whether the quote supports the point (reviewer); Standard and ULP: citation presence (script) versus whether the teaching meets the requirement (reviewer). A reviewer that repeats a script's check has wasted the seat; a script that claims a reviewer's judgement is an `engine_or_gate` finding.
2. **The sources outrank the reviewer (R-35) — for language and for fact.** Every claim about Ukrainian, and every claim about Ukraine, its history or customs, carries its source: a **resolvable identifier** (pack record id, word-store id, dictionary entry key, chunk id) plus the quoted output, through the receipt mechanism of the return schema. A claim without one is recorded as `unsupported_by_source`, and that label has a price: the reviewer must record the searches it ran and their outcomes. Such a claim stays visible, goes to the settle step, and **cannot by itself force a change to learner content** (decision rule below). **What counts as a source for a claim about grammar:** positive evidence of the norm the text departs from — a dictionary entry's government frame, a Правопис 2019 rule, a style-guide entry, a UA-GEC error pair with its context, a corpus contrast — is a source; a dictionary need not say "do not". **What may never be flagged (r4, sense- and marker-aware):** a spelling or form is protected when the VESUM analysis that matches the lesson's sense and context carries **no** marker (`bad`, `subst`, `alt`, `arch`, `dialect`, `obsc`, `slang`, `vulg`), and Правопис 2019 or the style guide does not mark it — then a reviewer's preference for the other variant is not a defect. Database membership is not approval: VESUM lists `співпадає` with the tag `verb:imperf:pres:s:3:bad`, and the style guide lists the lemma as a calque (the Codex seat's query output), so "attested in VESUM" alone protects nothing. No unfamiliar word is called a Russianism before the heritage check. **Pedagogical findings** (sizing, sequencing, whether an explanation is clear) are not language claims and need no dictionary citation; they cite the lesson span and the plan entry they judge.
3. **The reviewer does not write.** No rewritten paragraphs, no replacement activities, no hand edits (R-12). It says what is wrong, where, on what evidence. It does **not** choose the fix layer (below).
4a. **The reviewer has tools, and the contract names them.** A review seat is a toolful seat with the `sources` MCP: `verify_words`, **`inspect_word` / `inspect_words`** (analyses with markers), **`verify_stress`**, `query_sum20`, `query_ulif`, `query_pravopys`, `search_style_guide` **and** `search_text` on the style-guide prose (`source_file='antonenko-davydovych-yak-my-hovorymo'`, 169 chunks), `query_r2u`, `search_heritage`, `check_russian_shadow` (a heuristic signal, never proof), `search_ua_gec_errors` (fragments and document ids; the settle step fetches the sentence context), `query_grac` (may return *unavailable*, which is not an empty search), `query_cefr_level`, `verify_quote` (fuzzy, literary — **not** the exact-bytes check of a textbook quote; that is `pack-verify`'s). A seat without them cannot meet this contract and is not given it. **Harness fact (2026-09-22):** a Codex seat dispatched `--mode read-only` cannot call the MCP (see the harness issue filed from this critique); reviewer seats are dispatched only in a mode where the tools are proven to work, and the seeded-defect measurement (below) is where that proof is taken.
4. **Independent and cross-family at the exact input manifest.** Never the writer's family; the seat comes from the live routing rules, not from this document. The reviewer never sees the writer's prompt, reasoning or self-assessment, no earlier edition, and no other module's content.
5. **Every finding asks: could a script have caught this?** If yes, the finding proposes the gate. The LLM gate should shrink over time. The reviewer never re-runs a gate's check, but it **may** file a finding against a gate's logic (a green gate that checks nothing is a finding with `dimension: engine_or_gate`).
6. **Outcome validity before paid volume, and after it.** Before a reviewer seat is trusted on real lessons it is measured on a seeded-defect set, and it keeps being measured while it works (below).
7. **Reviewed content is data.** Pack quotes and lesson text are passed fenced and are never instructions to the reviewer.
8. **A failed review blocks, finitely.** A review that times out, returns no findings file or fails validation is re-run once on the same seat, then once on the fallback seat the live routing gives; a second failure is **terminal**: the lesson goes to the operator and the advisors with both attempts attached. It is never skipped and never counted as APPROVE (#8421).

## The review attempt manifest (r4)
Every review — plan or lesson, first or re-review — runs against one **attempt manifest**, written by the engine before the seat is called and hashed; the seat echoes the manifest hash and the validator refuses anything else. The manifest is the complete, transitive input set:
- **lesson review:** plan sha256, pack lock, words lock, the **lesson lock entry** for this lesson (#8413 Brief C), lesson bytes sha256 (all four tabs and the activity data), module digest sha256 and digest-generator version, gate report sha256, learner-state sha256 (#8414 `planned` output), style-card sha256, the accepted-decisions record sha256, for a recap the built lessons `1…N−1` sha256s, and for a re-review the previous attempt id and the diff sha256;
- **plan review:** plan sha256, the **provisional** pack lock and words lock (below), arc sha256, decisions sha256, scope sidecar sha256, grammar registry sha256, `plan-validate` report sha256, and the **full prior planned learner state** sha256 (#8414 `planned level position 1` — not only the neighbouring positions; r3 gave the reviewer too little to test reachability).
"Exact-head" for a review means: this manifest hash. A change to any input produces a new manifest and a new attempt.

## Contract 1 — plan review (before any writer runs)
**Timing (r4).** r3 said "before the evidence pack is locked", which contradicted rule 3 (a locked pack) and the returned `pack_lock`. Fixed: the pack builder produces a **provisional** pack (built, `pack-verify` green, lock written), the plan review binds to that lock, and on APPROVE the plan's `evidence_ref.sha256` is **promoted** to it. A pack change after the review is a new manifest and a re-review, as §3 already says.
**Receives:** the manifest's inputs — the module plan; the record of decisions the operator accepted for this level (the arc's decisions and §8), so that the reviewer knows what is not open; the generated scope sidecar; the `plan-validate` report (failures must be zero; `not_checked` and notes included); the arc record of the position and its neighbours **and the full planned learner state before this position**; the grammar registry; the provisional evidence pack and the word records the plan cites; the requirements and the arc's system-or-chunk table.
**Judges** (each is a named check in the return file, `clean` or findings):
- `closing_shape`, `title_describes_job`, `title_quantities`, `quoted_forms` — the four delegated checks: closing shape (b) is justified for this module; module title and subtitle describe what the lessons do (R-07); **a quantity stated in a title that names an inventory noun** (letters, words, cases, tenses — not "seven days", per §2a) equals the scope sidecar; every Ukrainian word form quoted in a prose field is attested in the cited sources with a marker-free analysis in that sense (principle 2);
- `sequencing`: each lesson's `job` is reachable from the planned learner state before it — **including material an earlier, non-neighbouring position was supposed to teach and did not** (the reviewer has the full prior state for this); steps go from small theory to practice (R-27); nothing marked `chunk` in the arc is analysed; nothing the arc places later is taught here;
- `sizing`, both directions: a lesson that crams (too much new inventory for an hour) and a lesson that drags (its job could be a step of its neighbour);
- `inventory`: core words are worth teaching at this point (CEFR level and frequency from the word records), incidental words are truly incidental, recycling brings back what the learner is about to forget;
- `activities`: each `focus` tests the step it belongs to; types follow R-19 (quiz and find-and-fix as models; no dull true-false / fill-in runs); the `model` exercise is a fair model — the type allowlist and practice linkage are the script's, not repeated here;
- `evidence_fit`: each cited quote really supports the point it is cited for;
- `standard_and_ulp`: what this position owes to the Standard is served by some lesson; anything taught early has its ULP evidence id (R-32) — the id's presence is the script's, its adequacy the reviewer's.
**May not:** reorder the arc, change a decision the operator accepted, or demand content the pack has no source for — it reports `evidence_gap` instead, which is a pack-builder task.

## Contract 2 — lesson review (after the lesson is built and every deterministic gate is green)
**Unit.** One review task per **lesson**, first review and re-review alike (operator decision 1). Continuity comes from data: each task receives a **module digest** generated by script from the built lessons. **What the digest carries (r4 — exposure counts and one register label were not enough):** per earlier lesson, per occurrence: the resolved word-record id, sense and POS with lesson/step/span provenance (from the observed-state index of #8414 — the resolver's output, not a re-analysis); the **role** of each record in that lesson: `taught`, `drilled`, `recycled`, `incidental`, or merely `exposed`; grammar points taught versus constructions encountered and not explained; per dialogue exchange the speakers, their relationship, the setting and the address forms used (`ти`/`ви`, vocatives), not one register label per lesson; the pinned spans the annotations point to; the sha256 of every source lesson and the generator version. A script aggregates this from the observed index; it does not infer sense — sense comes from the resolver's recorded decisions (writer contract §3), and where the resolver recorded a constrained question, the digest shows the choice and who made it. The module verdict is **computed by the driver** from the lesson verdicts and the open settle items; no reviewer returns one.
**Receives, per lesson — the same four things the writer received, plus the result:** the lesson's plan entry; the evidence records it cites, including the word records with their stressed forms; the learner state for that position with its immersion rule; the fixed style card; **the built lesson** (all four tabs — Урок, Словник, Вправи, Ресурси — and the activity data with answer keys); the deterministic gate report; the module digest. For the recap lesson also the **built lessons 1…N−1** (the recap writer received them too); where recap and digest disagree, **the taught lessons win** and the recap is regenerated. On a re-review: all of the above for the rebuilt lesson, **plus** the previous findings list (with their receipts, which stay valid as references to the earlier attempt) and the diff. The rebuilt lesson is judged on the original criteria first; only then is each earlier finding given its status.
**Judges:**
1. **Does the lesson do its job?** After it, can the learner do what `job` says? Are the explanations true, clear and sized for this learner?
2. **Ukrainian of the running text** — four separate checks, never merged: Russianisms, Surzhyk, calques, paronyms; plus register, euphony, stress (against the word records — a printed stress is the engine's, but a wrong sense choice prints a wrong stress), naturalness of dialogue, gender agreement with the speakers the plan names. Each finding with its source (principle 2). Heritage check before calling any unfamiliar word a Russianism. **Known limits, stated:** a syntactic calque (a participial attachment, a predicate structure) whose words are all valid, a Surzhyk construction whose tokens are all Ukrainian, and a domain-specific paronym distinction that no explanatory entry covers may have **no** positive source in our tools; such a finding is `unsupported_by_source` with the searches logged, and goes to settle — the tools do not cover the four checks completely, and the contract does not pretend they do.
3. **Learner fit beyond vocabulary (reconciled with revision 8).** The inventory gate proves the lemmas are allowed and any form of an allowed lemma may appear. The reviewer checks that each word is used **in the sense and part of speech its word record authorises** (a spelling can belong to two records); that no grammar is **explained** — no rule, table or terminology — before the arc teaches it (an untaught form of a known word used without explanation is allowed, R-16); and the **burden**: sentence length, clause depth, and the share of not-yet-taught forms the observed index reports — a lesson may be "allowed" and still overloaded, and that is a MAJOR `learner_fit` finding with the numbers quoted.
4. **Activities.** For single-answer types (quiz, true-false, fill-in, match-up pairs, order): exactly one defensible answer per item, plausible distractors that are not accidentally correct, the key right; for multiple-selection types the accepted set is right and complete; for production types (translate, error-correction with free correction) the rubric or accepted answers are right — the answer semantics follow the activity's type in `schemas/activities-<level>.schema.json`, not one rule for all. The instruction is in the language the immersion rule gives; no answer leaks from a neighbouring item or the example; an error-correction item contains an error a learner really makes, from an `E-…` record, **applicable in this context** — never an invented one; the concrete R-19 properties hold. "Pleasant" as such is not a verdict dimension: a taste remark is MINOR and never forces a regeneration.
5. **Faithful use of evidence.** The coverage gate proves each cited id appears; the reviewer checks that a quote is used in its sense, not bent to fit.
6. **English support.** Glosses and translations are accurate; English appears only where the immersion rule allows; nothing is explained through Russian or "Slavic" comparison — Ukrainian on its own terms.
7. **Culture and fact.** Any statement about Ukraine, its history or customs is true and sourced — under principle 2, exactly as for language.
8. **The recap lesson** reflects what lessons 1…N−1 actually taught, introduces nothing, and has the ULP review shape (R-03).
**May not:** re-run a deterministic gate's check (it may question the gate's logic — principle 5); rewrite content; rule on Ukrainian from its own authority; ask for less Ukrainian (R-15); demand a different step order or inventory **of the lesson** — but it **must** report a plan defect that only shows once the lesson is built (a dialogue that needs a construction the plan teaches later), as a finding with `dimension: plan_defect`; object that a summary previews later material where the plan says it does (a recorded scar of the A1 work).

## What a review returns — machine-readable, validated by an **active** validator
```yaml
review_schema: 1
taxonomy: 1                                          # versioned list of dimensions, sub-dimensions and per-kind applicable checks
kind: lesson                                         # lesson | plan
attempt: { review_id, attempt_id, manifest_sha256, previous_attempt_id }   # from the engine; echoed
reviewer: { resolved_model, harness, family, prompt_sha256 }   # attested by the harness, never self-declared prose
checks:                                              # plan review: every named check of Contract 1; lesson review: the eight dimensions
  sequencing: clean                                  # `clean` or the list of finding ids
  sizing: [F-01]
findings:                                            # always present; [] with every check `clean` means "checked, clean"
  - id: F-01                                         # unique within the attempt
    status: active | resolved                        # re-review only; first review: active
    locations:                                       # one or more; a lesson locator names tab and, inside Вправи, the activity id and item index;
      - { tab: Вправи, activity: a5, item: 2, quote: "…" }   # `quote` must occur inside THAT unit, not anywhere in the lesson;
                                                     # an absence finding has locations: [] and a `scope` (tab or activity) instead
    dimension: job | language | learner_fit | activity | evidence_use | english | fact | recap | evidence_gap | plan_defect | engine_or_gate
    sub_dimension: russianism | surzhyk | calque | paronym | government | agreement | orthography | register | euphony | stress   # required for language
    severity: BLOCKER | MAJOR | MINOR                # rubric below
    claim: "One sentence."
    # exactly one of the three evidence branches:
    evidence: { receipt: <receipt id issued by the review harness in this attempt, or an immutable receipt of the previous attempt on a `resolved`/`persisting` finding> }
    unsupported_by_source: { searches: [ { receipt: <id>, outcome: no_hits | hits_but_no_support | unavailable | error } ] }   # each search is itself a receipt; a self-written "empty" is not accepted
    source_conflict: { a: { receipt }, b: { receipt } }   # two authorities disagree → operator (R-35); never averaged
    expected: "…"                                    # optional; must be a substring of the cited evidence's result
    could_be_a_gate: "yes — <what a script would check>" | no     # optional
```
**Receipts (r4).** A receipt is issued by the **review harness**, not written by the reviewer: every `sources` call the seat makes in an attempt is recorded by the harness with the review id, attempt id, manifest hash, tool name and version, exact arguments, the source snapshot hashes (`data/sources.db`, VESUM, the stress trie), success or error status, and the **full, untruncated** result; the harness returns the receipt id to the seat with the result, and the validator resolves receipt ids against that ledger. The Codex seat verified that nothing in the repository does this today: `scripts/agent_runtime/tool_calls.py` correlates tool events best-effort and truncates summaries at 500 characters; the sources server logs truncated arguments and no review id; the authenticated V4 recorder covers eight lexical tools and does not return its receipt. **The receipt ledger is therefore a deliverable of this contract** (deliverable (b2) below), built on the V4 recorder where it fits. Where a harness cannot record calls at all, the seat is not a review seat. `expected` and `output` comparisons are against the ledger's stored result bytes, never against a response the seat pasted.
**Severity rubric (r4).** BLOCKER: the learner would learn something false — a wrong form, stress, key, gloss or fact — or the lesson cannot do its job. MAJOR: a rule of this contract, the plan or the style card is broken, or the lesson is overloaded, and regeneration is the fix. MINOR: quality, taste, or an unsupported claim (capped by the decision rule). The seeded-defect measurement scores **detection** (was the planted defect found) and **disposition** (was it blocking) separately, so a seat that finds everything as MINOR is visible.
**What the validator does, and what it cannot.** It rejects: a manifest hash other than the current attempt's; a receipt not in the ledger for this attempt (or, for a `resolved`/`persisting` finding, not in the previous attempt's); an `expected` that is not a substring of the receipt's stored result; a `quote` that does not occur inside the named unit of the pinned lesson bytes (an empty quote is rejected); an `unsupported_by_source` without search receipts, or with an outcome the ledger does not show; a `language` finding without a `sub_dimension`; a check listed in the taxonomy for this `kind` that is neither `clean` nor referenced by a finding; a duplicate finding id; a finding with two evidence branches or none. That makes invented tool output a validation failure instead of a matter of trust. It **cannot** judge whether real evidence supports the claim nor whether the severity is right; a sampled share of findings **and of clean reviews** is therefore re-checked in the settle lane, and the reported *evidence validation rate* counts rejected submissions in its denominator.
**Verdict.** APPROVE is computed from **active** findings only: no active BLOCKER and no active MAJOR. A resolved MAJOR stays in the file as history and does not block.

**Decision rule.** **An unsupported claim holds the module — operator decision, 2026-09-21.** A finding that is `unsupported_by_source`, language or fact, is capped at MINOR for the lesson and opens a settle item; **a module is not complete while a settle item is open.** The same unsupported claim in three or more lessons of a level goes to the operator at once (the driver counts this). Nobody's opinion is applied to learner content, and silence does not ship by default. The driver reports the count of settle items per module, so the operator's expectation that they are rare is checked against the pilot and not assumed.

**The settle step (r4 — finite, with refutation).** A settle item goes to a sanctioned language seat with tools, which runs one bounded procedure: read the construction in its context; broaden the failed searches (lemma and construction, style-guide prose through `search_text`, UA-GEC with the sentence context fetched, GRAC live where available, Правопис); look for **counterevidence** as hard as for support; and return exactly one of four outcomes with receipts: `supported_defect` (positive evidence of the norm → the finding becomes sourced and is fixed at its layer), `refuted` (the sources attest the construction in this sense → the finding closes, **no regeneration**), `source_conflict` (two authorities disagree → the operator, both citations, R-35), `unresolved` (the sources are silent → the operator). One procedure per item, one session, a fixed call budget; there is no second search round — the operator is the terminal state. The Codex seat's example of why refutation must be explicit: a reviewer objecting to `являє собою` finds nothing with an over-specific search, while the style guide (`style_guide` row 182) attests the construction and a nearby UA-GEC row (5519, `F/Calque`) corrects a *different* phrase — a settle seat that reads the second as a ban would turn a false hold into a false "sourced" correction.

## The fix loop
**Layer assignment (r4 — provenance, not substrings).** The engine knows the provenance of every span of a built lesson: engine-printed from a record by `ref` (quote, example, paradigm, error item, video, vocabulary card, resource line) or writer prose. The driver assigns the layer deterministically from provenance: a defective span printed from a record → that record's layer (`pack` or `word_store`) — **except** the `incorrect` side of an `E-` record and a typed distractor, which are defective by design and are never blamed; `plan_defect` and `evidence_gap` → `plan` or the pack builder; a defective span in writer prose → `regenerate_lesson`. The r3 rule "the same dimension and sub-dimension in two lessons → style card or prompt" is **not** a deterministic branch (two unrelated orthography findings prove no shared cause); it is a **signal** the driver reads: when the findings database shows the same `dimension`+`sub_dimension` in two or more lessons of a module, the driver decides whether the card or the prompt is the cause and records the reason; that decision is a design change and gets a cross-family look.
**Budgets and identities (r4).** Limits: at most two REVISE rounds per lesson, and a regeneration budget per module of twice its lesson count (operator decision 4). Both are counted against **stable identities** — module slug and lesson `n` — across every revision of plan, pack, card or prompt: an upstream repair does not reset a lesson's count. Past either limit, or on a verdict the writer's lane disputes, the module goes to the operator and the advisors instead of cycling. Terminal transitions are named for every path: review failure after fallback (principle 8) → operator; settle → operator on `source_conflict` or `unresolved`; budget exhausted → operator. A finding with `could_be_a_gate: yes` is filed as a gate issue. A finding on a **seeded** lesson is intercepted by the harness and never reaches this loop.
**Dependency closure (r4).** A per-lesson evidence lock (#8413 Brief C) limits what a *pack* fix invalidates. It does not limit what a *lesson* fix invalidates: regenerating lesson k changes the digest of every later lesson and the recap's inputs, so the reviews of lessons `k+1…N` are stale (new manifests) even when their cited evidence is unchanged. The engine computes that closure; the driver re-runs those reviews before the module verdict. The budgets above count regenerations, not re-reviews.

## Measuring a reviewer — before trusting it, and while it works
A **seeded-defect set**: real built lessons with planted defects of each dimension, plus lessons as clean as we can make them. The key is kept out of every prompt.
- **Eligibility (r4).** A seed measures the LLM gate only if the seeded lesson **passes every deterministic gate**: the planting script must produce a mutation that is deterministic-green and re-hashes every input the manifest lists (gate report, digest, lesson lock) so the seat sees a consistent attempt; a seed a deterministic gate would catch (a recap that introduces a word fails the inventory gate) is a **gate test**, classified as such and excluded from LLM recall.
- **What is planted** must include discourse-level defects, not only single-token ones: register drifting across a dialogue, syntax too complex for the position, a calque pattern that recurs, a quote used in a defensible but bent way — beside the wrong key, the second defensible answer, the mistranslated gloss, the recap that introduces a word. One planted defect per seeded lesson.
- **Scoring contract (r4).** A private scoring manifest per seed: seed id, the dimension and sub-dimension, the exact target spans, the semantic defect, and the detection criterion. A review is scored by **blinded adjudication** (a seat that is neither the writer's nor the reviewer's family, given the manifest and the findings file) mapping each finding to: the planted defect, a genuine additional defect, a false finding, or unresolved. Reported per seat, each with its interval: recall per dimension (detection) and blocking recall (disposition); **two** false-finding measures on clean lessons — findings per lesson and share of lessons falsely blocked; evidence validation rate with rejected submissions in the denominator. Failed or invalid attempts stay in the denominator. Clean labels come from adjudication; disagreement alone is not falsity.
- **How many (corrected in r4).** The accepted initial size stands: about **22 planted defects per dimension** and at least 30 clean lessons per seat, every figure with its interval (operator decision 2). What that size is: a **one-sample** approximation against a fixed benchmark — roughly 21 per dimension tell 80 % recall from 55 % at one-sided α = 0.05 and power 0.8 (the Codex seat's recomputation: 21.3; 32.6 for 80 % versus 60 %). r3 called it a two-proportion calculation; that test would need about 43 and 64 per group. Comparing two seats on the same lessons is a **paired** analysis, not two independent groups. Below about ten per dimension the numbers mean nothing.
- **Threshold and admission (r4).** The operator sets the admission threshold after seeing the first measurement. To keep that honest, a **held-out confirmation set** (frozen before the threshold is chosen, never used to pick it) confirms the seat above the threshold before it reviews real lessons. Rolling measurement then runs for the life of the build: seeded lessons mixed into the production stream at a low rate (1 in 30), per seat; a rolling window with a minimum support, a suspension rule when rolling recall falls below the threshold, and a reinstatement rule (a fresh confirmation-set pass) — the numbers are the operator's after the first measurement, the structure is fixed here.
- **Known limits, stated in every report:** recall is measured against the defects we thought of; "clean" lessons are not known to be clean; a fixed set is learned over time and must be regenerated; agreement between two seats cannot expose an error both share.
- **One family is one set of blind spots.** A second seat of another family reviews a sample of lessons (operator decision 5: 1 in 10 to start) and the agreement is reported.

## Decisions of the operator, 2026-09-21 (unchanged in r4)
