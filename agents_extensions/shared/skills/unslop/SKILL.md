# Unslop

Edit the draft. Preserve the meaning and the evidence. Rule numbers are stable. A removed rule leaves a gap.

## Content

3. Delete a dangling "-ing" phrase that adds no source ("highlighting", "ensuring", "fostering"), or replace it with the fact.
5. Delete "experts believe" unless you name the source.

## Language

7. Replace showcase vocabulary (delve, pivotal, tapestry, underscore, vibrant, landscape used as an abstraction) with a plain word.
8. "Is" or "has", not "serves as" or "boasts".
9. State the point. Do not write "not just X, but Y".
10. Do not force ideas into groups of three.
11. Repeat the precise noun. Do not cycle synonyms.
12. Do not write "from X to Y" unless X and Y share a scale.

## Style

13. Avoid the long dash. Use a period or a comma.
14. A colon introduces a list or an example, not a second clause.
15. Do not bold every name.
16. A bold label that restates the sentence is a tell. A label that names a new fact is fine.
17. Sentence-case headings.
18. No decorative emoji.
19. Straight quotes.

## Artifacts and filler

20. Delete chatbot closers ("I hope this helps", "certainly").
22. Do not praise the question. Answer it.
23. "To", not "in order to". Delete "it is important to note that".
24. One hedge is enough.
25. End on a fact or a next action, not a slogan.

## Plain speech

26. Prefer the concrete word over an abstract metaphor noun.
27. Say what the reader can do or check. If the sentence could sit unchanged in another project's docs, cut it.
28. One idea per sentence.
29. Active voice unless the actor is unknown.
30. Cut an adverb or use the measured number.
31. "Use", not "utilize". "Help", not "facilitate".
32. No aphorism where a literal phrase exists.
33. Write whole sentences, with articles and verbs. Do not leave arrow chains or dropped words.
