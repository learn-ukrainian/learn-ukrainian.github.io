<TabItem label="Урок — Lesson">

This lesson brings all conversational pieces together in live dialogue. At the
A1.1 stage, communication is practical, friendly, and grounded in standard
phrases. You will practice an authentic exchange between two students meeting
for the first time, deliver your own self-introduction monologue, and observe
etiquette in modern text chat.

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- maintain a complete peer introduction in the **ти**-register from greeting to
  farewell;
- ask and answer questions about origin, student roles, and family;
- deliver a coherent five-sentence monologue introducing yourself;
- greet and introduce yourself politely in written online chats.

## Діало́г

This is the full first-contact task. Read it once for meaning, then read it
again aloud. The setting is a break on the first day of Ukrainian courses, so
both people use **ти**.

Notice how smoothly the conversation flows from greeting to name, origin,
profession, and family. Direct address uses comfortable vocative forms:
**Богда́не!** and **Соломі́є!**. When responding to **Ду́же приє́мно**, the natural
Ukrainian answer is **Мені́ теж** (me too).

Use this as your graduation speech for A1.1:

- **Приві́т! Мене́ зва́ти Оле́на.**
- **Моє́ прі́звище Ме́льник.**
- **Я з Кана́ди. Я кана́дка.**
- **Я студе́нтка. Моя́ майбу́тня профе́сія — лі́карка.**
- **Моя́ ма́ма — вчи́телька, а мій та́то — інжене́р.**
- **У мене́ є одна́ сестра́.**
- **Ду́же приє́мно познайо́митися!**

If you want a shorter answer, keep five sentences: name, origin, role, one
family line, and closing. That is enough for the checkpoint.

:::tip
Your five-sentence answer can stay simple. If it has **Мене́ зва́ти...**,
**Я з...**, one identity line, one family line, and a polite closing, it is doing
the job.
:::

### Пе́рше знайо́мство в ча́ті

A modern first contact may happen in a course chat. The same etiquette applies:
start with a greeting, name yourself, ask one clear question, and close
politely. Do not hide behind emoji-only messages when the task is language
practice.

English support after the Ukrainian chat:

| Украї́нська | English support |
| --- | --- |
| **А тебе́?** | And you? |
| **Приві́т, Лі́но!** | Hi, Lina! |
| **Мені́ теж.** | Me too. |

This online task also reviews the borrowed-word habit from the wiki brief:
modern Ukrainian can use international words, but your core first-contact
grammar should stay Ukrainian. In a course chat, still say **Мене́ зва́ти...**,
**Я з...**, and **До поба́чення**.

## Слу́хай, зо́шит, підсу́мок

For lawful listening, use Ukrainian Lessons Podcast Episode 10 as listen-only
review support. Listen for short phrases you already know, repeat them, and
return to this page. Do not copy or transcribe paid material.

For handwriting recognition, ask a teacher, tutor, or classmate to write an
original five-line card with **ім'я́**, **прі́звище**, **Кана́да**,
**сестра́**, and **До поба́чення**. First compare the notebook card with the
printed model; then write your own final card.

Listening often helps you catch the natural rhythm of full sentences and
keep vowels clear without blending them. In handwriting, Ukrainian cursive
forms for letters like **в**, **д**, **т**, and **м** differ distinctively
from their printed counterparts. Writing cards by hand helps you recognize
them quickly when reading and write them smoothly yourself.
