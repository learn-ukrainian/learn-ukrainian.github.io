## Textbook Check

### Розми́нка та повто́рення — Warm-up and Retrieval

Before opening the textbook rules, let us retrieve the golden foundation you have built. In Ukrainian schoolrooms, teachers write one fundamental distinction on the board from the very first week:

> **Зву́ки ми чу́ємо й вимовля́ємо, а бу́кви ба́чимо й пи́шемо.**
> *(Sounds we hear and pronounce, while letters we see and write.)*

A sound (**звук**) belongs to your voice, your breath, and your ears. A letter (**лі́тера** or **бу́ква**) belongs to paper, screens, ink, and your eyes. Letters are visual signs that represent spoken sounds.

Recall the core numbers that define the Ukrainian language:

| Concept | Number | What it means for you |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Лі́тери (Letters)** | **33** | The entire Ukrainian alphabet (**абе́тка**). |
| **Зву́ки (Sounds)** | **38** | The actual speech sounds heard and pronounced in living speech. |
| **Голосні́ зву́ки (Vowel sounds)** | **6** | The open, singing sounds formed by voice alone: **[а], [о], [у], [е], [и], [і]**. |
| **При́голосні зву́ки (Consonant sounds)** | **32** | The sounds formed with voice and noise or noise alone, shaped by lips, teeth, and tongue. |

Why are there 38 sounds but only 33 letters? Because Ukrainian letters work efficiently: some letters (such as **Я, Ю, Є, Ї**) can represent combinations of sounds, many consonants come in paired hard and soft varieties, and the soft sign (**ь**) carries no sound of its own.

### Презента́ція — Presentation: The Textbook Standard

Let us review the four major phonetic benchmarks that Ukrainian textbooks emphasize. When you master these four points, your Ukrainian pronunciation and reading will stand on solid ground.

#### 1. Terminology: Why We Never Say «Голосна́ лі́тера»

In everyday English, people often casually say "vowel letters" and "consonant letters." However, Ukrainian philologists and school textbooks (such as Litvinova and Vashulenko) strictly warn learners: **ніко́ли не говорі́ть «голосна́ лі́тера»**.

Why is this rule so strict? Because letters are silent graphic symbols. An ink mark on paper cannot sing, hum, or vibrate vocal cords. Only a living human sound can be a vowel (**голосни́й звук**) or a consonant (**при́голосний звук**). Therefore, Ukrainian textbooks always use the precise formula:
- **лі́тери, що познача́ють голосні́ зву́ки** — letters that represent vowel sounds (there are 10 such letters: **А, О, У, Е, И, І, Я, Ю, Є, Ї**);
- **лі́тери, що познача́ють при́голосні зву́ки** — letters that represent consonant sounds (there are 22 such letters);
- **знак м'я́кшення (ь)** — a graphic sign that has no sound of its own and only softens the consonant before it.

#### 2. Pure Vowels: No Vowel Reduction (Нія́кого а́кання)

In English, unstressed vowels frequently blur into a vague, lazy "uh" sound. In Ukrainian, vowels hold their authentic quality regardless of stress:

- **Clean [о]**: Ukrainian **о** never turns into [а]. In the word **молоко́ — milk**, all three syllables contain a pure, rounded **[о]**: say **мо-ло-ко́**, never *ма-ла-ко́*. In **До́брого ра́нку! — Good morning!**, keep both unstressed **о** sounds round and clear.
- **Strict separation of [и] and [і]**: In Ukrainian, **[і]** is a high front vowel made with a smiling mouth, while **[и]** is an open-mid front vowel made with relaxed lips. Swapping them alters the meaning entirely:
  - **дім — house / home** (with **і**) vs **дим — smoke** (with **и**)
  - **ліс — forest** (with **і**) vs **лис — fox** (with **и**)
  - In your greeting **Приві́т!**, the first syllable has **[и]** and the second has **[і]**.

#### 3. Energetic Consonants: Voiced Endings Never Devoice

Ukrainian school textbooks (such as Kravtsova, Grade 4) state a binding orthoepic rule: **дзвінкі́ при́голосні зву́ки в кінці́ сло́ва та скла́ду вимовля́ємо дзві́нко** (voiced consonant sounds at the end of a word or syllable are pronounced with voice).

If you let your vocal cords relax at the end of a word, you change the word itself:
- **гриб — mushroom**: keep the final **[б]** vibrating and voiced. If you devoice it to *[п]*, you say **грип — flu**!
- **сад — garden**: finish with clear voiced **[д]**, not voiceless *[т]*.
- **лід — ice**: finish with clear voiced **[д]**.
- **зуб — tooth**: finish with clear voiced **[б]**.

#### 4. Special Letters: Ь, Ї, and The Contrast of Г vs Ґ

- **Ь (м'яки́й знак)**: Produces no sound. In **день — day**, your tongue touches the roof of your mouth to soften **н** into **[н′]**, and then you stop. Do not add an extra vowel sound after it.
- **Ї**: Always represents two sounds: **[й] + [і] = [йі]** in all positions. It never softens preceding consonants.
- **Г vs Ґ**:
  - **Г г** is a voiced pharyngeal or glottal fricative **[г]**, an airy throat sound as in **гора́ — mountain** or **го́лос — voice**.
  - **Ґ ґ** is a voiced velar stop **[ґ]**, an explosive plosive sound identical to the English *g* in *garden*, found in words like **ґу́дзик — button** and **а́ґрус — gooseberry**.

### Пі́дсумок мо́дуля — Module summary

You have completed your first module in Ukrainian. You did not begin by memorizing complex grammar tables or foreign transliteration schemes; instead, you started with living sound, connecting your ears, vocal cords, and eyes to the authentic patterns of the language.

By the end of this module, you can:

- greet others warmly and politely in formal and informal settings with **Приві́т!**, **До́брий день!**, **До́брого ра́нку!**, and **До́брий ве́чір!**;
- ask **Як спра́ви?** and answer naturally with **До́бре**, **Чудо́во**, or **Норма́льно**, adding the friendly reciprocal **А у тебе́?**;
- introduce yourself and ask a partner's name with **Як тебе́ зва́ти?**, **Мене́ зва́ти...**, and the reciprocal **А тебе́?**;
- say farewell using **До поба́чення!** and **На все до́бре!**, and express pleasure at meeting with **Ра́дий тебе́ ба́чити** (male) or **Ра́да тебе́ ба́чити** (female);
- explain the golden rule of Ukrainian literacy: **зву́ки ми чу́ємо й вимовля́ємо, а бу́кви ба́чимо й пи́шемо**;
- recognize all 33 letters of the Ukrainian alphabet (**абе́тка**), identify the 6 core vowel sounds (**[а], [о], [у], [е], [и], [і]**), and understand that a syllable (**склад**) needs a vowel sound;
- protect your pronunciation against beginner traps by keeping unstressed **о** clean in words like **молоко́**, preserving voiced word endings in **гриб** and **сад**, and treating the soft sign (**ь**) as a silent modifier.

Keep the scope small. You are not expected to read fluent literary texts or analyze complex sentence syntax today. You have acquired the indispensable phonetic instincts, letter awareness, and everyday greeting formulas that make all future Ukrainian learning clear, enjoyable, and authentically grounded.
